# UNINTENDED — Playable Density Strategy

## Purpose
Balance the systemic vision with enough actual play that players stay because of lived situations, not merely because the premise is unusual.

This is deliberately **not** a quest-count strategy. Do not solve content depth by adding 10 main quests, 5 side quests and infinite NPC repeatables.

## Core playable rhythm

Every meaningful local matter should be capable of moving through:

1. **ARRIVAL** — something feels wrong, but the player lacks context.
2. **DOUBT** — two pieces of evidence no longer fit comfortably together.
3. **INVESTIGATION** — the player forms and tests a theory using places, people, objects or records.
4. **INTERFERENCE** — the player changes something and becomes part of the evidence.
5. **AFTERMATH** — the world reacts; the local matter becomes clearer but a larger question remains.

AFTERMATH is not "quest complete". It is the point at which the world has changed enough to create future consequences.

## Density target

A five-location Map should eventually support roughly:
- 3–5 unresolved local matters at different maturity stages;
- 2–4 meaningful reasons to revisit each location;
- NPC knowledge that changes with relationship/history;
- ordinary objects that participate in multiple systems;
- at least one situation where a player's own action becomes evidence;
- at least one social reason to compare results with another player or Map;
- at least one unresolved question that survives logout.

This is a density target, not a visible task count.

## Player journeys

### 1. The Investigator
Arrival: notices an inconsistency.
Doubt: obtains conflicting testimony or records.
Investigation: follows an evidence chain across several locations.
Interference: deliberately changes possession, identity, sequence, route, record or obligation.
Aftermath: the Map now contains evidence created by the player; another contradiction becomes visible.
Retention hook: "I know more than I did, but I made the problem stranger."

### 2. The System Breaker
Arrival: tests grammar and obvious boundaries.
Doubt: discovers a refusal that varies by context.
Investigation: maps which conditions alter the refusal.
Interference: causes a bounded anomaly or fictional Server incident.
Aftermath: receives consequence, alias, reputation/history, or a retained exception.
Retention hook: "There is another rule I have not yet managed to offend correctly."

### 3. The Social Researcher
Arrival: asks NPCs and players what they think is happening.
Doubt: learns that another witness or Map reports a different result.
Investigation: compares evidence rather than following a prescribed solution.
Interference: shares, trades, lends, announces or participates in somebody else's test.
Aftermath: relationship/history/social reach changes and a collective theory emerges.
Retention hook: "Someone else got a different result from mine."

### 4. The Collector / Custodian
Arrival: notices an object with unusual provenance.
Doubt: ordinary ownership rules behave differently around it.
Investigation: learns custody through experimentation and community history.
Interference: borrows, trades, protects, steals, returns or fails to transfer it.
Aftermath: custody becomes public history and creates new social pressure.
Retention hook: "Where is it now, and what did the new owner learn?"

### 5. The Casual Explorer
Arrival: observes a place and somebody interesting.
Doubt: receives one understandable contradiction rather than a wall of lore.
Investigation: follows whatever caught attention, with temporary assistance if needed.
Interference: makes one consequential choice using an ordinary action.
Aftermath: receives a clear world reaction plus one unresolved lead.
Retention hook: "I can leave now, but I want to see what that changed."

## Rules for repeatability

Do not use static repeatable quests as the default retention mechanism.

Repeatable activity is acceptable when at least one of these changes:
- target;
- consequence;
- social participant;
- location state;
- relationship state;
- economic condition;
- available evidence;
- Map interpretation;
- anomaly possibility.

If the same NPC can request the same action with the same consequence indefinitely, it is maintenance content, not world simulation.

## World reactions over rewards

Prefer:
- changed NPC testimony;
- altered access;
- new social relevance;
- custody consequences;
- institutional response;
- changed environmental state;
- new contradiction;
- retained exception;
- reputation/history;

over generic currency or XP.

Rewards can exist, but should usually change what the player can attempt rather than merely increasing a number.

## Pixel / app migration compatibility

Text remains the authoritative intent and simulation layer.

Future pixel presentation should render existing state rather than replace it:
- character position represents current location/sub-location;
- NPC sprite/state represents availability, relationship and profession;
- item sprite represents authoritative entity ownership/location;
- structures represent institutions/projects/property;
- weather/light/environment represent world flags and anomalies;
- dimensional effects represent projected zone state;
- movement controls emit the same server-authoritative intents as text commands.

The browser version should therefore be capable of evolving from terminal-first to hybrid text/pixel without rewriting core rules. Mobile apps later consume the same APIs and event model.

## Inspiration research, 2021–current

Research was used only to identify structural patterns. Do not copy characters, scenes, terminology, plots, powers or prose.

Useful patterns:
- worlds that require the protagonist to learn and obey hidden rules create durable curiosity;
- constrained or locked capability is more engaging than immediate universal power;
- professions become interesting when they are lived work with consequences rather than menu-selected classes;
- knowledge from a prior/system perspective is compelling when the world can still surprise or punish the knowledgeable person;
- institutional/guild systems create stakes when they control resources, access and social legitimacy rather than existing as a chat tag.

Failure warnings observed across weaker/stalled system stories and criticism:
- one-directional mission systems become bland when every problem is simply accepted and completed;
- convenient rewards that appear exactly when needed erase investigation and uncertainty;
- generic rank/level escalation can consume the premise until worldbuilding becomes decorative;
- giving the central figure unrestricted creation/system authority removes meaningful friction;
- repeating the same mission structure with larger numbers creates volume without depth.

UNINTENDED correction:
- no omnipotent player system;
- no guaranteed solution item;
- no visible mission treadmill;
- no universal chosen-one arc;
- knowledge can be wrong because Maps disagree;
- exceptions are bounded and often inconvenient;
- professions/guilds emerge from institutions and work later, not character creation;
- world reaction is the primary content multiplier.

## Content acceptance test

Before adding any new location, action, NPC or artifact, ask:
1. What existing systems does this interact with?
2. Can a player form more than one theory about it?
3. Does player interference produce a consequence?
4. Can another player/Map observe or interpret it differently?
5. Does it create a reason to return?
6. Does it remain useful once the initial mystery is known?

If fewer than three answers are strong, deepen an existing piece of content instead of adding another noun.
